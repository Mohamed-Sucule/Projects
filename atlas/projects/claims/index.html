Claims Desk

Reads an insurance claim — email, scanned form, invoice — checks it against the policy and itself, and recommends a decision with the evidence for every line.

Launch Claims Desk ↗ Engineering case study Source

The problem

Claims handlers spend their day retyping paperwork and checking it by hand.

Evidence

11 of 11 fields read from each scan; on 180 smudged scans a misread only ever sends a claim to a person. Read its tests

What it does

  • Reads scanned forms and invoices with OCR, in the browser
  • Runs up to thirteen checks, each linked to the field or clause it rests on
  • Recommends approve, decline or refer, and records who approved or overrode it

The most interesting decision

A misread can only ever send a claim to a person

Across 180 deliberately smudged scans the tests enforce a rule stricter than any accuracy figure: a misread may refer a claim, but must never pay one that should not be paid or decline one that should not be declined.

Where the AI is, and where it isn’t

The model

  • Tesseract OCR, running in WebAssembly and served from this site, reads the scans into words with their positions on the page

Ordinary code

  • Turns the OCR text into facts, allowing for OCR's usual slips (£ read as E, O as 0)
  • Runs up to thirteen checks against the policy and between the documents, each citing a field or a clause
  • Recommends approve, decline with the clause quoted, or refer to a person

What keeps it honest

  • 180 deliberately smudged scans on every test run: a misread may send a claim to a person, but must never pay or decline wrongly
  • Records who approved or overrode the recommendation
  • A referral asks for something and never uses the words fraud, suspicious or investigation

Not built yet

  • Images only: no PDFs or multi-page documents
  • Typed forms only: handwriting would need a handwriting model and a person checking it

Built with

  • Tesseract OCR in WebAssembly
  • OCR-tolerant field reader
  • Thirteen policy checks
  • Web Worker
  • Human override
