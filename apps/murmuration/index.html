Murmuration

Starlings over the reeds at dusk. You are the falcon.

A flock of thousands of starlings swirls over a reedbed as the sun goes down, folding and stretching as it turns. Moving the pointer, or pressing Send the falcon, sends a falcon at them, and the flock opens around it. After about two and a half minutes the light goes and the whole flock drops into the reeds for the night.

This browser cannot run the full flock on its graphics card, so this is a smaller one, flying by the same rules.

Seven neighbours

A murmuration has no leader. Each starling watches its six or seven nearest neighbours and does three things: turns to fly the way they fly, closes on them, and keeps its distance. Researchers who filmed real flocks in Rome found it is seven birds, not every bird within some distance, which is why a flock can stretch thin and still hold together.

Every bird here follows those rules, all of them at once, sixty times a second, on your graphics card. Finding the seven nearest of tens of thousands of birds for every bird would be too slow even there, so the sky is cut into boxes at three sizes. Each frame every box counts the birds in it and adds up where they are and which way they are going. A bird looks at the smallest boxes around it that hold about seven others.

Two more rules: stay over the reedbed where they will sleep, and get away from the falcon. Only the birds nearest the falcon can see it, but their neighbours turn with them, and theirs with them. The turn spreads across the flock as a wave of dark and light, which is what you see in a real murmuration when a falcon goes through.

There is no animation here, no image and no sound file. The birds, the sky, the reeds and the sound are all made as you watch.

Down for the night.

The whole flock went into the reeds in about thirty seconds, as the light went.
