Ember: an action game where your light is your life

One number is both how hurt you are and how far you can see. Everything else in Ember follows from that: the lighting, the fights, and the tests that make sure the dark is never unfair.

The one rule

Your ember is your health and your light radius at once.

At 100 you can see across a room. At 20 you can see your own feet, and the eyes in the dark are all that tell you where things are. Cinders, dropped by everything you kill, feed it back; a flare spends it to burn everything near you and relight the braziers. That one number turns every fight into a decision about how much light you can afford.

You feel how hurt you are before you read it on the meter, in how far you can see: the room tells you first.

Light you can feel

The light is a visibility polygon. Rays are cast from the flame to every wall corner within reach, the nearest hit on each is kept, and the shape they make is punched out of a layer of darkness with a radial gradient. So every wall and pillar casts a real shadow, and the shadows swing as you move. The braziers' shadows never move, so they are worked out once and kept; yours are worked out again every frame.

The tests check the light the way a player would: light stops at walls, and the floor behind a pillar is dark while the floor in front of it is lit.

Fair by construction

A game played half in the dark has to be scrupulous about what it hides. So every attack in Ember is either a hazard drawn on the floor or a shot with a wind-up, and both carry the moment they were first shown. When something hits you, the game records how much warning you had.

The tests then play the whole game on three seeds and assert that every hit, every attack and every shot was shown at least 0.3 seconds before it could land. The tightest in the game is the King's slash straight after his dash, at 0.38 seconds.

Bosses written as scripts

The three bosses are generator functions: yield 0.8 means "wait 0.8 seconds". An attack reads like a stage direction: show where it will land, wait, land it, send the shockwave out, recover.

// play/ember/engine.js: the Warden's slam, from his script (the second phase's extras left out)
const warn = 0.85 * tempo;
b.face = Math.atan2(ty - b.y, tx - b.x); b.pose = 'raise';
const hz = g.hazard({ shape: 'circle', x: tx, y: ty, r: 86, warn, dmg: 24, by: 'slam' });
yield warn;
b.pose = 'slam';
g.shake = 12;
g.emit('slam', { x: tx, y: ty });
g.hazard({ shape: 'ring', x: tx, y: ty, speed: 250, w: 16, maxR: 320, warnAt: hz.warnAt, dmg: 14, dur: 9, by: 'shockwave' });
yield 0.6;
b.pose = 'idle';

Their phases are branches in the same script: the Warden's temper, the Choir grieving as its masks fall, the King's eclipse and his last light. Writing a fight this way keeps its rhythm in one place, where it can be read and tuned.

A map that is a journey

Each act grows a tree of rooms across a 5 × 4 grid, favouring the deepest branch, so an act is a path with side rooms rather than a hub you keep returning to. The boss goes in the deepest dead end and the shrine in another. The nine ash-stones, which answer the question on the title screen (who put out the Beacon?), go in the rooms least on the way, so finding them all means choosing to look.

Found by playing it

An autopilot plays from the first room to the ending, beating each boss through every phase. Sweeping it across many seeds found things no hand-written test would have:

FoundWhat changed
A mite and the player, pinned foreverOn 4 of 40 seeds a run never finished: the last enemy and the player were stuck against pillars on opposite sides, each walking straight at the other. Enemies now follow a shortest-path map of the room whenever something is in the way.
Two masks dying in the same breathThe Choir only noticed deaths between songs, so two masks falling in one attack skipped its last phase. A mask's death is now announced the moment it happens.
Summoned into a pillarThe Warden calls mites in around him, and sometimes "around him" was inside a column. Everything is now placed on the nearest open floor.

Checked

  • Every room, brazier and ash-stone of every act is reachable, on 40 seeds, and the boss is always in the deepest dead end.
  • The autopilot finishes the game on every seed tried, every boss goes through every phase in order, and all nine ash-stones can be reached.
  • Nothing hurts you without warning, and nobody, you or an enemy, ever ends up inside a wall.
  • The same inputs play out exactly the same way.
  • Dashing makes you untouchable, Gentle halves blows, and cinders refill your ember but never past the brim.
  • Dying wakes you at the last lit brazier; dying to a boss resets the boss.
  • The King takes half damage in the dark and full damage in a brazier's light, and his last words agree with the ledger of ash-stones you found.
